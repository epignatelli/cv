\noindent\textit{Re: RL Fellows Program}
\vspace{3em}

\noindent To whom it may concern at \textit{Anthropic},
\vspace{2em}

\noindent
I am writing to apply for the \emph{RL Fellows Program} at \textbf{Anthropic}.
\emph{My research} sits at the intersection of deep RL and large language models, with a focus on \emph{credit assignment} and building \emph{scalable RL environments}.
I combine an academic track record in RL/LLMs with five years of industry experience shipping ML systems, and I am motivated by the challenge of \emph{scaling RL} to produce safe, capable AI — which is precisely what this Fellowship is designed to accelerate.

\paragraph{Academic and research experience.}
Currently, I am an \emph{Assistant Professor in Machine Learning at UCL}.
I lead three modules: \textit{ELEC0136: Machine Learning System Design}, \textit{ELEC0021: Programming for Machine Learning}, and \textit{ELEC0054: Research Projects}, where I coordinate UCL's thesis-project portfolio.
Seven months in, I was nominated for the \emph{UCL Inspiring Teaching Delivery award}.

Before this, I completed my PhD at \emph{UCL} under the supervision of Laura Toni and Tim Rocktäschel at the intersection of \emph{Deep Reinforcement Learning} (RL) and \emph{Large Language Models} (LLMs) with a focus on the Credit Assignment Problem.
I reviewed the field with \textit{A Survey on the Temporal Credit Assignment Problem}, which received the TMLR ``Survey Certification'' \emph{award} for \textit{exceptional thoroughness}.
I showed that \emph{Large Language Models} align with human evaluations of action relevance across navigation tasks, and I developed \emph{NAVIX}, a \emph{widely adopted} JAX reimplementation of MiniGrid that enables faster iteration and cleaner evaluations.
Most recently, I co-authored \emph{BALROG} (ICLR 2025), a benchmark for evaluating LLM and VLM agents on long-horizon interactive tasks.

\paragraph{Research agenda.}
I care about \emph{scaling RL} to real-world problems through principled environment design and credit assignment.
A key conclusion from my doctoral studies is that effective RL training demands two things: \emph{environments that expose long-horizon structure}, and \emph{training schemes that propagate credit across it}.
My current work targets this directly: I am developing \emph{latent option models} that scale \emph{options} — temporally extended actions — to large, complex environments. By learning a reusable vocabulary of latent actions from large-scale, \emph{action-free} demonstrations, agents acquire a discrete action space that can then be fine-tuned with RLHF/RLAIF.
This connects directly to the Fellowship's workstreams: multi-turn reasoning and long-horizon tool use in Claude require RL signals that propagate meaningfully across hundreds of steps — the harder the credit assignment problem, the more principled the environment and training scheme need to be.

\paragraph{Industry experience.}
Before academia, I spent \emph{five} years translating early machine learning research into commercial products.
At Burohappold (2017 to 2020, London, UK), as \emph{Machine Learning Lead}, I delivered deep-learning tools now used by the \emph{Premier League} and internal teams, creating new revenue streams and increasing operational efficiency.
At Gridshell.it (2015 to 2017, Naples, Italy) I developed open-source multi-objective \emph{genetic algorithms} for structural and acoustic engineering in resource-constrained settings; this work was recognised with the \emph{Peter Lord Award}.

These roles developed my ability to align scientists, engineers, and stakeholders around ambitious, complex deliverables, and strengthened my \emph{systems} mindset—building reproducible codebases and infrastructure in \emph{JAX}/\emph{PyTorch} for long-running experiments.

\paragraph{Why me?}
I have a rare combination of \emph{RL research depth}, hands-on \emph{JAX environment-building} experience, and a proven ability to \emph{ship production ML systems}.
My work on NAVIX shows I can build robust, scalable RL infrastructure: thousands of parallel environments on a single GPU, adopted across multiple research groups.
My credit assignment research shows I understand the hard theoretical problems that sit beneath effective RL training.
\emph{My agenda}—principled environment design and temporally extended action learning to scale RL—\emph{aligns} closely with the Fellowship's goal of improving Claude's capabilities through RL.

Five years of \emph{industry experience} gave me a product-oriented, demand-driven mindset and the organisation and management skills to \emph{plan effectively} and \emph{execute efficiently}.
My \emph{working ethos} is grounded in teamwork, collaboration, and mutual understanding.

The \emph{transition from architecture} to AI was a \emph{proactive} leap that shows \emph{flexibility}, rapid skill acquisition beyond my comfort zone, and sustained \emph{determination} and \emph{dedication}.
In short, this multidisciplinary journey equipped me to manage and execute \emph{complex}, \emph{ambitious} projects that span multiple fields.

\paragraph{Why the RL Fellows Program at Anthropic?}
Anthropic is doing the most \emph{ambitious RL-for-alignment} work in the field, at a scale and pace that no academic group can match.
The Fellowship offers direct mentorship from researchers defining the frontier of post-training, access to compute that would compress years of progress into four months, and a \emph{clear path} — 25–50\% of Fellows receive full-time offers — to contributing to that mission long-term.
I thrive where the challenge is not between a bad and a good idea, but between a \emph{good} and a \emph{great} one — settings that keep my motivation high and maximise the impact I can deliver.
